Why we built AURA

Smartwatches are bulky, dashboards you can't ignore, and dead by lunch. AURA strips a wearable down to the one thing people actually keep on 24/7 — a ring — and gives it superpowers.

Solar-Assist Charging

A photovoltaic micro-cell in the band trickle-charges from ambient light and hand motion, stretching a full charge from 3 days to roughly 7 under normal indoor/outdoor use. You still dock it monthly — you just stop thinking about it weekly.

How AURA actually gets manufactured

No mystery box. Here is the real supply chain, step by step — the same path you'd follow to build a private-label version of a product like this yourself.

1

Design & Reference Sourcing

Industrial design finalized in CAD (Fusion 360 / SolidWorks), then matched to an existing smart-ring ODM/OEM platform — dozens of factories in Shenzhen and Dongguan already produce a base smart-ring module (PPG+SpO2 sensor stack, BLE chip, 20–40mAh battery) that can be customized with your shell, firmware skin, and app branding.

2

Component Sourcing

Titanium shell blanks (CNC-machined), sensor PCB modules, solar micro-cells, and batteries are sourced from separate specialist suppliers found via Alibaba, 1688.com, and Global Sources, then consolidated at the assembly factory. Typical minimum order quantity (MOQ) for a private-label run: 300–500 units.

3

Assembly & Firmware Flashing

Ring bodies are laser-welded, sensor modules are potted for waterproofing, and each unit is flashed with signed firmware and paired to a unique BLE ID during final assembly and calibration.

4

QC & Certification

Batch sampling against AQL 2.5 defect tolerance, plus the certifications you actually need to sell legally: CE, FCC, RoHS, and IP68/10ATM water testing. Budget 3–5 weeks and $2,000–$6,000 for lab certification on a first run.

5

Freight & Duties

Air freight for the first 500-unit batch (fast, ~7–10 days) or sea freight once you scale past a few thousand units (cheap, ~30–35 days). Duties depend on destination country and HS code (wearables typically 0–6%).

6

Fulfillment

Inventory lands at a 3PL (e.g. ShipBob, Amazon FBA, or a local fulfillment partner) which picks, packs and ships direct to customers as pre-orders convert to sales.
